Your Education.Your Choices.Your Future.

The Freedom Academy is an upper school built on one belief: no one is born with a finished identity. Students here discover who they are by choosing what they study, how they learn, and what they stand for, and they take ownership of every one of those choices.

Mission

We give every student the freedom to explore their interests, the courage to shape their own identity, and the responsibility to own the choices that make up their education.

I

Encourage independent thinking

Students question ideas instead of memorizing answers. Every course includes Socratic seminars where students argue, test and defend their own conclusions.

Measured by: a defended position paper each term
II

Promote personal growth & creativity

Each student designs a year-long Passion Project and keeps a reflective journal tracking how their thinking and identity change.

Measured by: an annual portfolio & Authorship Showcase
III

Develop responsibility through choice

Students write their own learning plan, the Path Contract, and review it with a mentor. Freedom comes with accountability for outcomes.

Measured by: Path Contract reviews every six weeks

Our Philosophy · Existentialism

We don't decide who students become. They do.

Existentialism holds that people are not born with a fixed purpose. We create who we are through the choices we make, and we are responsible for them. A school built on this idea cannot hand every student the same script. It has to give them room to choose and the support to choose well.

“Existence precedes essence.” Jean-Paul Sartre · Existentialism Is a Humanism, 1946
PrincipleWhat existentialism saysWhat we do about it

Freedom

The idea

Human beings are free to define themselves, and no outside authority should define them in advance.

In practice

Students choose 60% of their timetable: electives, a self-designed Passion Project, and how they demonstrate learning.

Responsibility

The idea

Freedom is not an excuse. We answer for what our choices create.

In practice

Every student signs a Path Contract, sets their own deadlines and reflects on results with a mentor. When choices fail, they revise them instead of being punished.

Authenticity

The idea

Living by values you have examined, rather than by what others expect of you.

In practice

No class rank or letter grades to chase. Students receive narrative feedback and are assessed against their own goals.

Meaning

The idea

Life has no pre-packaged meaning. Each person must create it.

In practice

Daily reflective journaling, a core course on the big questions, and the humanities and arts at the heart of the curriculum.

Our Campus · Physical Environment

A campus designed for choosing, not just for sitting.

Every space answers the same question: where does this student learn best right now? Students move freely between quiet rooms, loud studios and open outdoor space. There are no bells and no hall passes.

The Passion Project 20% OF THE WEEK

Each year, every student designs a personal project around a question they care about: launching a business, writing a novel, building a robot, running a community campaign. With their mentor, they set the goal, the plan and the success criteria, then present it at the Authorship Showcase.

Teaching Methods & Strategies

Mentors guide. Students lead.

Socratic Seminar

Students sit in a circle and answer open questions with evidence and reasoning. The mentor asks questions instead of giving answers.

Project-Based Learning

Learning happens through real projects with real audiences, chosen and planned by students.

Independent Inquiry

Students pose their own research questions and decide how to investigate them.

Reflective Journaling

Daily writing on what was chosen, what happened and what to try next. This is self-assessment in practice.

Choice Boards

Students show mastery in the form they choose: an essay, a podcast, a model, a performance or a debate.

Dilemma Labs

Students role-play real ethical dilemmas, make a decision and live with its consequences in the simulation.

School Rules

Three principles. Students write every other rule.

i.

Own your choices.

You are free to choose, and you are responsible for what follows. No excuses, no blaming others, and always the chance to choose again.

ii.

Protect others' freedom.

Your freedom ends where it limits someone else's. Respect, safety and consent are non-negotiable.

iii.

Speak honestly.

Authenticity starts with honesty: about your work, your effort and yourself. Disagreement is welcome when it is respectful.

The Hidden Curriculum

What students learn without being told

Your time is your responsibility.
With no bells, students learn to plan, prioritize and keep commitments.
Your voice changes things.
When students write the rules, they learn that their community is theirs to shape.
Mistakes are part of becoming.
Narrative feedback and revision show that failure is information, not a verdict.
Authority is earned through dialogue.
Mentors sit in the circle, use first names and explain their reasoning.
You are not a number.
With no rank and no curve, students compare themselves only to who they were.
Self-schedulingStudents plan their open blocks each Monday and share the plan with their mentor.
Keeping commitmentsA chosen commitment is a promise to your team.
Asking for helpSeeking support is a sign of responsibility, not weakness.
Restorative repairBroken rules lead to a conversation and a plan to make things right, not punishment.

An honest challenge we plan for: complete freedom can overwhelm a 14-year-old. That is why freedom grows with the student. Ninth graders choose within guided options and meet their mentor weekly. Seniors design almost all of their own schedule.

Our Teachers · We Call Them Mentors

A guide beside the student, not a sage on the stage.

An existentialist teacher doesn't decide who a student should become. They ask the questions that help students decide for themselves, then support the path the student chooses.
